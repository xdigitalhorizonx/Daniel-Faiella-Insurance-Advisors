Life Insurance

Life insurance without the medical exam.

Dreading the needle — or worried a health condition will get you declined? More coverage than ever is now issued with no exam at all. Here's how the different no-exam paths really work in Nevada, what each one costs, and which one fits.

By Daniel J. Faiella · Licensed Insurance Advisor, Carson City NV · October 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Two things stop people from ever applying for life insurance. The first is the dread of the old-fashioned medical exam — the scheduled visit, the blood draw, the questions about every doctor you've seen. The second, and quieter, is the fear of being turned down: "I have high blood pressure, so why bother?" Both are solvable, and both have changed enormously in the last few years. A large share of policies today are issued with no medical exam at all, and even people with real health conditions have more doors open than they think.

But "no-exam" isn't one product — it's three different underwriting paths, each with its own trade-offs in price, coverage size, and who it's built for. Once you've worked out how much coverage you need and what type of policy fits, choosing the right underwriting path is what gets you approved at the best price your health allows.

Why the medical exam is fading

For decades, almost every life insurance application triggered a paramedical exam and lab work so the insurer could price your risk. That still exists, and for some people it's still the cheapest route. But insurers can now pull a remarkable amount of information instantly — prescription-drug histories, motor-vehicle records, and data from the Medical Information Bureau — and feed it through underwriting algorithms. If your digital record looks clean, the computer can price you accurately without ever sticking a needle in your arm. That shift created accelerated underwriting: a fully medical-grade decision, minus the exam.

The four underwriting paths, side by side

Every life insurance application you'll be offered falls into one of these four lanes. Knowing which one you belong in is half the battle.

Accelerated underwriting: the sweet spot for healthy applicants

If you're in reasonably good health, this is usually the path to want. You complete a full application — honestly and completely — and instead of sending you for an exam, the insurer verifies your answers against those instant data sources. Approvals can come back in minutes to a few days rather than the four-to-eight weeks a traditional policy takes, and the pricing is often within a hair of a fully underwritten rate. Coverage amounts can run well into seven figures. The one catch: if your records surface something unexpected, the carrier can "kick out" the application into traditional underwriting with an exam after all. That's not a rejection — it just means the fast lane didn't fit, and it's one more reason to apply through someone who knows which carriers' accelerated programs are most forgiving.

Simplified issue: skip the records, answer a few questions

Simplified-issue policies ask a short set of yes/no health questions — things like recent hospitalizations, cancer treatment, or certain diagnoses — but require no exam and don't dig deeply into your full medical file. Decisions are fast, and the questions are narrow enough that many people with well-managed conditions sail through. The trade-off is cost and size: you'll generally pay more per dollar of coverage than on an accelerated policy, and face amounts are capped lower. It's a strong fit when you want coverage quickly, have a condition that might slow full underwriting, or simply don't want your complete records pulled.

Guaranteed issue: coverage when you've been told no

Guaranteed-issue policies ask no health questions and cannot decline you within the eligible age range — typically designed for older adults or those with serious conditions. That certainty comes at the highest price per dollar of coverage, the smallest face amounts, and a graded death benefit: if you pass away from natural causes in the first two to three years, your beneficiary generally receives your paid premiums back plus interest rather than the full benefit (accidental death is usually covered in full from day one). It's a genuine safety net for final expenses when nothing else is available — closely related to the final expense coverage many Nevada seniors use — but because of the cost and the waiting period, it should be the last option you consider, not the first.

How to qualify well — even with a health condition

Getting the best outcome isn't luck; it's a few deliberate moves:

  • Answer every question honestly. A no-exam policy still relies on your word and a two-year contestability window. An omission discovered after a claim can cost your family the benefit — the exact opposite of why you bought it.
  • Shop carriers, don't settle for one. Underwriting is wildly inconsistent between companies. One insurer treats sleep apnea or a past DUI as a near-automatic decline; another barely blinks. Matching your history to the right carrier is where an independent broker earns their keep.
  • Lead with your strongest lane. Try accelerated underwriting first if you're reasonably healthy — only step down to simplified or guaranteed issue if you need to. Don't overpay for guaranteed issue if a few honest answers would have gotten you a far better policy.
  • Apply sooner rather than later. Age and new diagnoses only move premiums one direction. The healthiest and youngest you'll ever be for insurance purposes is today.

The Nevada details worth knowing

The products are national, but a few things work in your favor here. Nevada law gives you a free-look period after your policy is issued — a window to review it and cancel for a full refund if it isn't what you expected. The death benefit is income-tax-free at the federal level, and because Nevada has no state income tax, your beneficiary keeps every dollar — the same advantage that makes keeping coverage into retirement go further here. Just remember the two-year contestability period: during it, the insurer can review a claim against your application, which is precisely why honesty on a no-exam form matters as much as it would with an exam.

Good questions

No-exam life insurance questions, answered straight.

Is no-exam life insurance more expensive?

It depends on the type. Accelerated underwriting — the modern no-exam path for healthy applicants — often prices very close to a fully underwritten policy because the insurer still reviews prescription, driving, and medical-database records. Simplified-issue and guaranteed-issue policies, which ask fewer or no health questions, cost more per dollar of coverage because the insurer takes on more unknown risk. The healthier you are, the smaller the price gap.

Can I get life insurance in Nevada if I have a health condition?

Usually, yes — the question is which door you go through. Many well-managed conditions, like controlled high blood pressure or diabetes, can still qualify for standard or lightly rated coverage, sometimes even no-exam. More serious or recent conditions may route you to simplified-issue or guaranteed-issue policies, which accept you with few or no health questions in exchange for a higher premium and smaller face amount. Because each carrier underwrites differently, an independent broker can match your history to the company most likely to say yes.

What's the difference between simplified issue and guaranteed issue?

Simplified-issue asks a short set of yes/no health questions but requires no medical exam, and can decline you based on your answers. Guaranteed-issue asks no health questions at all and cannot turn you down within the eligible age range, but it carries the highest cost, the smallest coverage amounts, and a graded death benefit — meaning if you pass away from natural causes in the first two to three years, your beneficiary generally receives your premiums back plus interest rather than the full benefit.

How fast can no-exam life insurance be approved?

Much faster than traditional coverage. With no paramedical exam to schedule and no lab work to wait on, accelerated and simplified-issue decisions can come back in as little as a few minutes to a few days, versus the four to eight weeks a fully underwritten policy often takes. The exact speed depends on the carrier and how cleanly your records come back.

Does living in Nevada change my options?

The products are national, but Nevada adds protections and advantages. Nevada law gives you a free-look period after your policy is issued, during which you can cancel for a full refund if it isn't right. The death benefit is income-tax-free federally, and Nevada has no state income tax, so your beneficiary keeps the full amount. A two-year contestability period also applies, which is why answering every health question honestly on even a no-exam application matters.
